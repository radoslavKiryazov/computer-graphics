Reflections

To position and orient the cubes, I used translation matrices $ \mathbf{T}(t_x, t_y, t_z) $ to shift vertices, and rotation matrices $ \mathbf{R}_x(\theta) $ and $ \mathbf{R}_y(\theta) $ to revolve them around the X and Y axes.

The virtual camera is defined using the look-at view matrix $ \mathbf{V} = \text{lookAt}(\mathbf{e}, \mathbf{c}, \mathbf{u}) $, which transforms world coordinates relative to the camera's eye position, center target, and up vector. For the actual projection, an orthographic matrix $ \mathbf{P}_{\text{ortho}} $ is used to create parallel lines for the isometric view, while a perspective projection matrix $ \mathbf{P}_{\text{persp}} $ creates depth scaling for the one-point perspective, two-point perspective, and three-point perspective views.

Finally, because WebGPU utilizes a $ [0, 1] $ depth range rather than the standard $ [-1, 1] $ OpenGL range, a depth adjustment matrix $ \mathbf{M}_{\text{st}} $ is consistently applied.

The composite Model-View-Projection matrix $ \mathbf{M}_{\text{MVP}} $ transforms each 3D vertex $ \mathbf{p} $ using the following equation:

$$ \mathbf{p}_{\text{clip}} = \mathbf{M}_{\text{MVP}} \cdot \mathbf{p} $$

Part 1: Isometric View

We relu on an orthographic projection. The model matrix remains the identity matrix $ \mathbf{I} $, and the final concatenation applies the WebGPU depth adjustment, the orthographic projection, the view matrix, and the identity model matrix in sequence:

$$ \mathbf{M}_{\text{MVP}} = \mathbf{M}_{\text{st}} \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{ortho}} \cdot \mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{I} $$

Part 2: Perspective Views

For the second part, the camera uses a perspective projection $ \mathbf{P}_{\text{persp}} $, and the scene draws three instances of the cube using different model transformations.

One-Point Perspective: Here I simply translated the cube along the X-axis using a translation matrix $ \mathbf{M}_1 = \mathbf{T}(-2.2, 0, 0) $. The concatenated matrix is:

$$ \mathbf{M}_{\text{MVP}, 0} = \mathbf{M}_{\text{st}} \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{persp}} \cdot \mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{M}_1 $$

Two-Point Perspective: To achive a two-point perspective I rotated the cube $ 45^\circ $ around the Y-axis. To rotate the cube around its own center $ \mathbf{c} $, it is first translated to the origin, rotated, and translated back, forming $ \mathbf{M}_2 = \mathbf{T}(\mathbf{c}) \cdot \mathbf{R}_y(45^\circ) \cdot \mathbf{T}(-\mathbf{c}) $. and arriving at:

$$ \mathbf{M}_{\text{MVP}, 1} = \mathbf{M}_{\text{st}} \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{persp}} \cdot \mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{M}_2 $$

Three-Point Perspective: To achive a three-point perspective, I first translated the cube to the right and rotated it around its center along both the X and Y axes. The resulting model matrix is $ \mathbf{M}_3 = \mathbf{T}(2.2, 0, 0) \cdot \mathbf{T}(\mathbf{c}) \cdot \mathbf{R}_x(35^\circ) \cdot \mathbf{R}_y(45^\circ) \cdot \mathbf{T}(-\mathbf{c}) $, giving us:

$$ \mathbf{M}_{\text{MVP}, 2} = \mathbf{M}_{\text{st}} \cdot \mathbf{P}_{\text{persp}} \cdot \mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{M}_3 $$
