Filtering & Wrapping Explanations

Address Mode (Wrapping):

  • repeat: UV values outside [0, 1] wrap around (dropping integer parts), causing the checkerboard texture to tile continuously across the quad plane.
  • clamp-to-edge: UV values outside [0, 1] clamp to the border texels, stretching the outer edge pixels across the remainder of the polygon.

Magnification (magFilter):

  • nearest: Active when texels are larger than screen pixels (up close). Picks the single closest texel center, producing sharp, blocky, pixelated square boundaries.
  • linear: Performs bilinear interpolation between 4 adjacent texels, producing a smooth, blurred transition between texels up close.

Minification (minFilter):

  • nearest: Active when multiple texels fit inside a single screen pixel (in the distance)[cite: 11]. Samples a single texel per pixel without averaging, causing severe moiré aliasing and flickering near the horizon.
  • linear: Bilinearly interpolates neighboring texels, slightly reducing high-frequency noise but insufficient on its own for severe perspective minification.

Mipmapping (mipmapFilter):

  • Disabled: Uses only Level 0 (64x64). Results in heavy flickering and noise at distant geometry.
  • nearest: Uses pre-calculated lower-resolution texture levels for distant pixels, eliminating aliasing. However, switching between mip levels creates visible seams across the ground plane.
  • linear (Trilinear): Interpolates smoothly between adjacent mipmap levels, removing visible level transitions across perspective depth.
